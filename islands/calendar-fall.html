Calendar Fall

K'in calls a day sign, and every stone that wears it falls.

The floor is a giant Mayan calendar, and the temple is counting the days.

K'in calls a day sign. Every stone carved with that sign shakes, then drops. The stone wheels click round under your feet, three floors stacked over a sacred well. Read the glyph, find a safe stone, stay up. Last Climber standing wins the day. K'in will call them. She is sorry in advance.

Genre
Party knockout, last one standing
Players
2–16; alone: a survival time trial
Round
Under 4 minutes, usually about 3
Map
The Counting Well: three calendar wheels over a sacred well
Tone
No weapons; touch the well and you're out for the round

Five day signs

A colour, a shape and a sound for every sign

Four are real day signs of the Maya calendar; K'ak', the Maya word for fire, is the fifth. Each glyph is carved deep enough to read as a shape even without colour.

A call

Read it, then move

The wheels click one step. K'in names a sign, its glyph lights up on the walls, and every stone that wears it glows and shakes. Then it drops, and whoever stood on it falls.

Falling a floor is not the end: you land on the wheel below, with one floor less between you and the well. Only the water puts you out.

A round

From dawn to eclipse

  1. Dawn

    One sign per call, slow, with a long warning.

  2. Midday

    The outer wheels start to turn, and every third call names two signs.

  3. Afternoon

    Two signs every call, and the wheels sometimes reverse.

  4. Eclipse

    K'in now calls what stays, not what falls: “Only Ajaw holds.” The top floors stop coming back.

A second chance

K'in's Feather

Every 30 seconds a golden feather appears. Hold it, and the next time you touch the well K'in catches you instead.

Out, not bored

The drum gallery

Players who are out drum for a sign from the rim and nudge which one K'in calls next.

1 player

Solo Survival

The same calls in the same order for everyone each update. Survive as long as you can.

Into the series

The Disc Well

The wheels come from the flagship's Disc Well in Tier III, and the five signs become the glyph language of its puzzles.
