Art & Assets

Art Direction

How the temple should look and feel, and the rules that keep it consistent.

Painterly, stylized, believable

The look sits in the register of Ratchet & Clank and Jak & Daxter: stylized and readable, but grounded. Colour is rich without being oversaturated, textures are soft and hand-painted, and shapes are chunky enough to read at a glance while you are jumping.

Seven materials, and nothing else

Carved pale limestone, jade inlay, hammered gold, glowing glyphs, mossy weathered stone, obsidian and torch flame. Nothing in the temple uses a material outside this list.

Four energy colours

Every interactive object is marked in one of four colours:

  • Gold: the sun
  • Blue: water
  • Red: fire
  • Green: growth

On top of that, jade means solvable, ember means it can kill you, and gold only ever means the way out.

Three lighting states

A room is dark before it is solved, saturated while it is running, and gold once it lets you through: unlit, charged, resolved. You can read your progress in a room from its light alone.

Two render styles

Characters are polished stylized 3D in a modern animated-film look, set on neutral grey sheets so they double as modelling references. The level scenes are painterly. See the cast lineup.

The guardians' sigils glow a cold silver-white, deliberately outside the elemental palette. Kan Uk is the exception: his burns a banked amber.

The screen palette

Six colours from the game's art bible, used for the interface and the level art.

  • Limestone#EFE6CE
  • Torch gold#F0B24A
  • Ember#DF4A2A
  • Jade#33C0A2
  • Wet stone#152230
  • Deep dark#070B10

From concept to level

The concept art is mood reference, not layout. Positions and sizes come from the level layout data, built on a 5.12 m grid with a 3.84 m storey height, so every painted room can be rebuilt to scale from the modular kits.
