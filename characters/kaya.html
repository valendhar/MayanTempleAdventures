The reader

Kaya

Twenty-three, a scholar's daughter, and the first climber in centuries who reads the wall instead of the stair.

Kaya survives by reading, not fighting. She arrives with her mother's notebooks, her own rubbing kit and a certainty that she will be the first living person to read the Spirit Mural.

She grew up in field camps, archive basements and the back rows of lecture halls, raised by an epigrapher whose theory the experts laughed at. She learned to take a rubbing, sketch a glyph and compare tool marks before she was a teenager.

Her mother vanished fourteen months ago without a body, a note or a witness. Kaya keeps a daily count of the days on the back page of her notebook, in her mother's bar-and-dot numerals, and speaks of her in the present tense on purpose. She has not told anyone, including herself, whether she has come for a mural or a mother.

In the game, you are Kaya. She isn't a fighter when she arrives, and she dies on the Shell's traps within the hour. She learns to fight because the temple gives her no other choice, and by the upper floors she fights like someone who has died a hundred times, because she has. Her goggles, her notebook and, later, a chisel are how she wins.

Role

Every step, from the first corridor to the Sky Altar

“Thank you. I mean that. And I'm going to check it anyway.”

Kaya

Design notes

Everything has to read in motion, at third-person range, against busy carved stone. Her cerulean tunic is the one cool colour on floors lit almost entirely by torch gold, so she never gets lost in a fire room. The braids hang straight down her back for clean 3D geometry, and goggles, satchel and braids stay in silhouette from every angle.
