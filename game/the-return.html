The Game

The Return

Wake on the stone floor. Pour the tea. Sell what you carried. Read what you copied. Go back up.

Dying is how the game moves forward. Every return brings something new: a line, a carving, a sale, a reading. You never go back up empty-handed.

The ritual

Back in the Threshold

The hall never changes, and that's the point. What changes is the record: what people say to you, what's on the table, and what's on the wall.

  1. Stone under her cheek

    She wakes on the Threshold floor. There's no wound, only an ache. The sunbeam has moved one seam. Another dot goes into the count on the back page of her notebook.

  2. K'in's greeting

    A line chosen by what just happened: a new killer, a new floor, a boss, or the same words worn smooth.

  3. Oxlahun's table

    “Tell me where it got you.” He pours tea, buys what you carried, and carves a little limestone keepsake of whatever killed you.

  4. The field desk

    Any Find can be studied here for Insight instead of sold. You can't do both.

  5. The Spirit Mural

    New panels light up. Kaya's own carving has changed. You work on your rubbings at the reading desk.

  6. The Memory Wheel

    Spend Spirit Fragments on permanent talents at the round altar.

  7. The portals

    Pick a Sun Gate or a lit shrine, a weapon and a keepsake, and go back up. It all takes about a minute, and you can skip any of it.

What death takes

The temple takes your gold. It never takes your notes.

Where you fell

The Chalk Mark

When Kaya dies, she leaves a chalk mark, one more dot in her count, drawn on the last safe ground she stood on. Reach it on your next run and touch it: every Find you held becomes clean again, and the coins you died with are back in your purse.

If you don't make it

Washed out

Die again before you reach it, and the mark is gone. What you held stays death-touched for good. The mark is never left inside a trap or a fight.

K'in

“You drew yourself a door, Professor.”

The souls-like hook, go back and win it back, without taking anything else away.

The trader

“Tell me where it got you.”

Oxlahun is really useful, and he has never once been wrong about a floor. His business also only works while climbers keep dying.

Every new thing that kills you earns a free carving of it. Equip one per run, and it helps against that killer. Dying somewhere new is how you get the tool against it.

Sell

Finds for Jade

Jade buys weapons, camp restoration and permanent stock. He always pays best for anything with writing on it.

Bounties

Three requests per return

“Two jade masks. No questions.” Double pay, and a reason to choose your next route.

Pawn

A loan against your gear

Pledge a weapon or keepsake for a large coin advance before a hard climb. Die, and the tab doubles.

The Waystall

A bell and a speaking tube

On the floors, you ring, a tray slides out, and his voice comes down the tube. Nobody knows how.

The tagged goods

A brass lantern, a notched staff

His table holds things that once belonged to someone. Each has a blank tag. You can buy them.

At every Portal Chamber

Climb on, or go home rich?

Risk it

Climb on

Keep everything and risk everything. Each floor without banking raises your Long Carry bonus on what you hold.

Pay for safety

Use the Drop

Pay part of your coins to send your Finds down the offering chute. They reach Oxlahun clean, and the bonus resets.

Walk away

Go home alive

Take the return portal. Your Finds are clean and the bonus pays out. Your blessings and coins are gone.

Sell or study

The decision behind every Find

Every Find can pay for your next upgrade, or teach you something. Sell it for Jade, or study it for Insight, which levels up the goggles and opens new readings. Reading the Temple

Home

A camp that fills up

Isabel's camp stands thirty steps from the hall. Jade restores it slot by slot, and each slot gives you something real.

SlotWhat it does
KettleTea before a run: extra health on the first floor
Map tableShows idols, side routes and evidence on each floor
Rope rackMore room in the satchel
Isabel's deskBetter study results, and her notebook pages

Permanent

The Memory Wheel

The round altar is a calendar wheel, and K'in tends it. Spirit Fragments buy lasting talents: a spirit K'in can catch once before it falls, more health, a second dodge, a keener eye. Every talent has two versions you can swap between.
